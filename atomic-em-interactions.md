## Electromagnetic Field Interaction Formulas

### Basic Interaction Principles

The interaction between electromagnetic fields and atomic systems is governed by several key equations:

1. **Lorentz Force**: The force experienced by a charged particle in an electromagnetic field
   
   $\vec{F} = q(\vec{E} + \vec{v} \times \vec{B})$
   
   Where:
   - $\vec{F}$ is the force vector
   - $q$ is the charge of the particle
   - $\vec{E}$ is the electric field vector
   - $\vec{v}$ is the velocity vector of the particle
   - $\vec{B}$ is the magnetic field vector

2. **Energy of an electron in an electric field**:

   $E = -e\vec{E} \cdot \vec{r}$
   
   Where:
   - $E$ is the energy
   - $e$ is the elementary charge
   - $\vec{E}$ is the electric field
   - $\vec{r}$ is the position vector

3. **Zeeman Effect**: Splitting of spectral lines in a magnetic field

   $\Delta E = \mu_B m_j g_j B$
   
   Where:
   - $\Delta E$ is the energy shift
   - $\mu_B$ is the Bohr magneton
   - $m_j$ is the magnetic quantum number
   - $g_j$ is the Landé g-factor
   - $B$ is the magnetic field strength

## Voltage-Impedance Relationships in Atomic Systems

### Conceptual Framework

When considering atomic systems in terms of electrical circuit analogies, we can model the following relationships:

1. **Electron Shell Impedance Model**:
   
   $Z_{shell} = R_{shell} + jX_{shell}$
   
   Where:
   - $Z_{shell}$ is the complex impedance of an electron shell
   - $R_{shell}$ is the resistive component (energy dissipation)
   - $X_{shell}$ is the reactive component (energy storage)

2. **Voltage-Current Relationship in Atomic Systems**:
   
   $V = Z_{effective} \cdot I_{electron}$
   
   Where:
   - $V$ is the potential difference (voltage)
   - $Z_{effective}$ is the effective impedance of the atomic system
   - $I_{electron}$ is the electron current (movement of charge)

3. **Impedance Scaling with Nuclear Charge**:
   
   $Z_{effective} \propto \frac{Z_{nuclear}}{\epsilon_r n^2}$
   
   Where:
   - $Z_{nuclear}$ is the nuclear charge
   - $\epsilon_r$ is the relative permittivity of the electron cloud
   - $n$ is the principal quantum number

## Mathematical Models for Electron Shell Behavior

### Quantum Mechanical Models

1. **Schrödinger Equation for Hydrogen-like Atoms**:

   $-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m} \nabla^2 \Psi + V(r) \Psi = E \Psi$
   
   Where:
   - $\hbar$ is the reduced Planck constant
   - $m$ is the electron mass
   - $\nabla^2$ is the Laplacian operator
   - $V(r)$ is the potential energy function
   - $\Psi$ is the wave function
   - $E$ is the energy eigenvalue

2. **Radial Wave Function for p-orbitals (relevant for B, N, F)**:

   $R_{21}(r) = \frac{1}{4\sqrt{6}} \left(\frac{Z}{a_0}\right)^{3/2} \frac{Zr}{a_0} e^{-Zr/2a_0}$
   
   Where:
   - $Z$ is the atomic number
   - $a_0$ is the Bohr radius
   - $r$ is the radial distance

3. **Electron Probability Density**:

   $\rho(r, \theta, \phi) = |\Psi(r, \theta, \phi)|^2$

### Semi-Classical Models for Electromagnetic Interactions

1. **Bohr Model with External Field Perturbation**:

   $r_n = \frac{n^2 \hbar^2 \epsilon_0}{\pi m e^2 Z_{eff}} \cdot \frac{1}{1 + \alpha E_{ext}}$
   
   Where:
   - $r_n$ is the radius of the nth orbit
   - $\epsilon_0$ is the vacuum permittivity
   - $Z_{eff}$ is the effective nuclear charge
   - $\alpha$ is the polarizability
   - $E_{ext}$ is the external electric field

2. **Electron Shell Response to AC Fields**:

   $x(t) = \frac{eE_0/m}{\sqrt{(\omega_0^2 - \omega^2)^2 + \gamma^2\omega^2}} \cos(\omega t - \phi)$
   
   Where:
   - $x(t)$ is the electron displacement
   - $E_0$ is the amplitude of the electric field
   - $\omega_0$ is the natural frequency of the electron
   - $\omega$ is the driving frequency
   - $\gamma$ is the damping coefficient
   - $\phi$ is the phase angle

## Theoretical Resonance Frequencies

Resonance frequencies represent the characteristic frequencies at which electrons in atoms respond most strongly to external electromagnetic fields.

### Fundamental Resonance Relationships

1. **Electronic Transition Frequency**:

   $\nu = \frac{|E_f - E_i|}{h}$
   
   Where:
   - $\nu$ is the frequency
   - $E_f$ is the final energy state
   - $E_i$ is the initial energy state
   - $h$ is Planck's constant

2. **Shell Resonance Frequency**:

   $\omega_{shell} = \sqrt{\frac{k_{eff}}{m_e}}$
   
   Where:
   - $\omega_{shell}$ is the angular frequency
   - $k_{eff}$ is the effective force constant
   - $m_e$ is the electron mass

3. **Cyclotron Frequency in Magnetic Field**:

   $\omega_c = \frac{eB}{m_e}$
   
   Where:
   - $\omega_c$ is the cyclotron frequency
   - $e$ is the elementary charge
   - $B$ is the magnetic field strength
   - $m_e$ is the electron mass

## Simulation Considerations

### Quantum Mechanical Simulation Approach

For accurate simulation of electron behavior in B, N, and F atoms under electromagnetic fields, consider the following methods:

1. **Time-Dependent Schrödinger Equation Solver**:
   
   $i\hbar \frac{\partial \Psi}{\partial t} = \hat{H} \Psi$
   
   With Hamiltonian including field terms:
   
   $\hat{H} = \hat{H}_0 + \hat{H}_{field}$

2. **Density Functional Theory (DFT) with External Field**:
   
   Calculate electron density $n(r)$ under influence of external potential $V_{ext}(r)$

3. **Monte Carlo Methods for Electron Behavior**:
   
   Simulate probabilistic electron behavior using Metropolis algorithm
